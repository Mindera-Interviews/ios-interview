# iOS Pair Programming Exercise — Brief

Welcome! This document explains what to expect from your technical interview session. Take a few minutes to read through it — we want you to feel prepared and relaxed on the day.

## What This Is

This is a **pair programming exercise** — not a take-home test, not a whiteboard session. You will work on a real iOS project, in your own development environment, while we collaborate with you in real time.

The goal is to see how you approach practical engineering problems: how you read unfamiliar code, how you reason through a problem, and how you communicate while building.


## Format

**Duration**: Up to 2 hours
**Style**: Pair programming (you drive)
**Environment**: Your own Mac, your own Xcode
**Language**: Swift

## Before the Session

1. Approximately 10 minutes before the interview, you'll be sent a link to download the project as a zip file
2. Unzip the project to a location on your machine
3. Make sure it runs and compiles (it should)
4. Have a quick read of the README.md file 

> The project targets **iOS 17** and requires **Xcode 18** or later.

## What to Expect

When the session begins, you will be given a short brief explaining what the app does and what you need to build. The project is a working skeleton — it compiles, runs, and displays content using mock data.

Your task will be to **extend the existing codebase** by implementing missing functionality. The code you write should fit naturally into the architecture and patterns already present in the project.

The session is structured roughly as:

1. **Orientation** (~10 min) — We walk you through the app, the architecture, and the requirements
2. **Implementation** (~60–80 min) — You implement the required features, with us alongside
3. **Discussion** (~20–30 min) — We talk through your decisions, trade-offs, and what you'd do differently

> It is not essential that all features be implemented, indeed it might not be possible in the time given.

## What We're Looking For

We're not looking for perfection or speed. We care about:

- **Reading unfamiliar code** — taking time to orient yourself is completely normal and expected
- **How you structure your solution** — does it fit naturally into the existing patterns?
- **How you handle uncertainty** — asking good questions and making reasonable assumptions are both signs of a strong engineer
- **Code quality** — readable, maintainable, and where possible, tested
- **Communication** — thinking out loud and explaining your choices matters as much as the code itself

You'll always impress us more by writing clean, well-reasoned code and explaining your thinking than by writing more code in silence.

> It may not be possible to complete all featuers in teh required time. Don't worry, this is not a speed run but a real-world process.

## What You Can Expect From Us

We won't be silent observers — we're in this with you.

- **No gotcha questions** — we're not here to trick you or catch you out.
- **Unopinionated** — we don't have a preferred technology or solution. We love any approach that has clear benefits and a well-reasoned rationale.
- **Friendly and relaxed** — we aim to create the same collaborative atmosphere that our engineering teams work in every day.

## Tools

### ✅ You May Use

| Tool | Notes |
|------|-------|
| **Xcode** | Any version ≥ 18 |
| **Google / Stackoverflow** | Any web resource you normally use. |
| **Old code** | You may use code you've written before. Just be prepared to walk us through what it does. |
| **AI** | AI tools are fine for looking things up or sense-checking ideas, but we'd ask you not to use them to generate code for you. The session is about seeing how *you* think, and AI-generated code makes that hard for both of us. |

### 💡 Tips

| Tip | Why it helps |
|-----|--------------|
| **Turn off Xcode Apple Intelligence** | Xcode tries really hard to complete your code for you, but this doesn't give you the opportunity to explain why you're writing it. |
| **Keep talking** | Tell us what you are doing, about to do, or why you did something. This helps us understand how you work and what you value in your code. We promise we will make it a 2-way conversation. |
| **Ask questions** | Asking questions is a completely normal part of pair-programming — we actively encourage it. |
| **Start simple** | Get something working before refining it. |
| **Don't over-engineer** | The skeleton has clear patterns; follow them. |
| **Tests are valued** | If time allows, writing tests demonstrates good instincts. |
| **It is fine to look things up** | We do it too — just explain what you're looking for and why. |
| **Third-party Swift packages** | Ideally you should be able to complete this assignment without 3rd party libraries. If you do use one, be prepared to explain why and what benefits it brings. |

## After the Session

Once we've finished coding together, we'll have a relaxed technical conversation. This is genuinely one of our favourite parts — there are no right or wrong answers, just a chance to talk through your experience. We might explore:

- Walking through the decisions you made
- Discussing what you would do differently with more time
- Talking about testing strategy
- Exploring edge cases you may not have had time to handle
- Discussing how the solution might evolve in a production app

It's a genuine conversation between engineers — not an interrogation.
